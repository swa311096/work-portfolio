# Compensation Design

Compensation design should match the work type. The key question is whether the output can be specified and verified cleanly, or whether the work requires ambiguity resolution, research, and iteration.

## When Is Paying Per-Task Better Than Hourly?

Per-task payment is better when:

- output is well-specified and easy to verify
- acceptance criteria are clear
- work has low variance in effort per unit
- the goal is to incentivize throughput and scale quickly
- quality gates, rejection rules, and redo rules can be applied cleanly

This works best for repeatable units of work where Mercor can define what a good submission looks like and quickly decide whether it passes. Examples include labeling, structured extraction, simple review tasks, unit-test-graded coding tasks, or standardized prompt-response creation.

The risk is that per-task payment can encourage speed over quality. It should only be used when there are strong quality checks and the definition of "accepted work" is clear.

## When Hourly Is Better Than Per-Task

Hourly payment is better when:

- tasks are ambiguous
- work requires research
- effort varies significantly across units
- the project needs collaboration, iteration, or deep investigation
- speed incentives could degrade quality

This fits work like rubric writing, taxonomy design, expert calibration, adjudication, failure-mode analysis, complex domain review, and early project scoping.

Hourly payment is useful when the expert is not just producing units of output, but helping shape the quality standard or resolve ambiguity.

## Interview Framing

I would use per-task payment when the work is repeatable, well-specified, and easy to verify with clear acceptance criteria. It is useful when we want throughput and can enforce quality through gates, rejection rules, and redo rules.

I would use hourly payment when the work is ambiguous, research-heavy, or highly variable in effort. For tasks like rubric design, taxonomy creation, adjudication, or deep investigation, hourly is safer because I do not want speed incentives to reduce quality.

The operating principle is simple: pay per task when output quality can be verified cleanly; pay hourly when judgment, iteration, and ambiguity resolution are the main value.
